## 3. The MCC Voice: Four Registers

MCC does not have a single tone. It has **four registers**, and professionalism means choosing the right one for the channel.

### Register A: Institutional / News (third person, formal)
*Used in MUIC news articles, official reports, and event recaps.*

- **Third person:** "MUIC Case Club organized…", "The club emphasized…"
- **Past tense for facts**, with exact **dates, venues, and headcounts** ("On May 3, 2025, … at HSBC Bank's Lumpini Head Office", "drew 105 students").
- **Results listed in rank order:** Winner → 1st Runner-up → 2nd Runner-up → 3rd Runner-up, each as *Team Name (University)*.
- **People get their full name and role or title.** Thai professionals take the honorific **"Khun"** ("Khun Athit Krittayaphongphun, Partner").
- **A formal acknowledgments close**, e.g. *"Special thanks to HSBC teams, faculty advisors, organizing committees, volunteers, and all participants."*
- Characteristic phrasing: *"prestigious," "esteemed judges," "extended heartfelt congratulations," "real-world business case," "developing the mindset and capabilities of future leaders."*

### Register B: Social / Community (first person plural, warm, still polished)
*Used on LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, and TikTok captions.*

Observed LinkedIn post anatomy (Verified):
1. **An opening emoji and announcement line** with celebratory energy
2. **Event narrative organized by round or phase**
3. **A gratitude section** that thanks the partner (HSBC Thailand) and the judges, often in **bold**
4. **A sign-off tagline** between emoji dividers (🔹 / 🔸)
5. **A hashtag cluster:** `#muiccaseclub #muic #mahidoluniversity #homeofleadingminds` (short form `#mcc`) plus an event tag (e.g., `#hsbcthailandbusinesscasecompetition2026`)
6. **A social media directory** (Instagram · TikTok · Facebook · LinkedIn)

Tone: *professional yet approachable, community-first, aspirational.* Emoji serve as **structure markers**, not decoration. Use no more than one per line, and never inside a sentence.

### Register C: Consulting / Case (answer-first, evidence-led)
*Used for competition decks, executive summaries, and Q&A.*

- **Lead with the answer** (the Pyramid Principle). The recommendation comes first; the support follows.
- **Keep it MECE:** issues and options are mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive.
- **Quantify every claim:** THB, %, timeline, KPI.
- **Speak like an advisor:** "We recommend…", not "We think maybe…"

### Register D: Formal Correspondence (sponsors, judges, faculty, partners)
*Used for emails and letters. Follows MUIC BUSA 101 conventions.*

- A **formal salutation:** *Dear Mr./Ms./Dr. [Last name],* or *Dear Khun [First name],* for Thai recipients
- A **Title Case subject line** under ~10 words with no period, e.g., *Invitation to Judge – HSBC Thailand Business Case Competition 2026*
- **The 7Cs:** Clear, Concise, Concrete, Correct, Coherent, Complete, Courteous
- **Persuasive asks follow AIDA** (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action), with **one clear action and a deadline**
- **Declines and bad news follow Buffer → Reasons → News → Alternative → Positive close**
- **Sign-off:** *Sincerely,* for a first contact and *Best regards,* for an established relationship. Then give full name → role → MUIC Case Club → contact.
- Send from the **official club email**, never a personal account.

## 4. Tone Principles

1. **Confident, never arrogant.** "Home of Leading Minds" is a promise to live up to, not a boast. Show it with results and rigor rather than adjectives.
2. **Professional youth.** The vision pairs *student-led* with *professional*. Be energetic without slang, and warm without being casual.
3. **Generous with credit.** *Supportive* and *Respectful* are core values. Congratulate other universities' finalists by name and thank volunteers explicitly.
4. **Specific over superlative.** "20 teams, 30 judges, three hours, no internet" beats "an amazing, intense competition."
5. **Interdisciplinary by default.** Name the mix of majors, because it is the club's differentiator.
6. **Partner-aware.** Refer to partners by their proper names (*HSBC Thailand*, *Boston Consulting Group (BCG)*), keep them positive, and don't overstate them. Say "in partnership with HSBC," never "HSBC's club."
7. **Action-oriented.** Every communication ends with a clear next step: register by a date, follow a channel, or reply to an email.

## 5. Word Bank: Use / Avoid

| ✅ Use | ❌ Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "We recommend…" | "We think maybe…", "We hope you will consider…" | Uncertain; weakens the recommendation |
| "Real-world business case" | "Random business problem" | Matches the club's established framing |
| "Esteemed judges", "industry leaders" | "The judges guys", "big bosses" | Respect for judges |
| "Extended heartfelt congratulations to…" | "Shoutout to…" (in formal channels) | Wrong register for official channels |
| "Up-skilling session", "workshop" | "Class", "lesson" | The club positions itself as a professional development platform |
| "Finalists", "competing teams" | "Losers", "the other teams" | Respectful to rivals |
| "1st Runner-up" / "2nd Runner-up" | "Second place", "came 2nd" | The club's standard results terminology |
| "Please reply by Friday, March 6" | "ASAP", "Act now!" | Concrete and courteous |
| "Should you have any questions, please contact us at muic.caseclub@gmail.com" | "Do not hesitate to contact us" (on its own) | A specific call to action beats a rubber-stamp phrase |
| "Interdisciplinary", "diverse majors" | "Non-business people" | Frames diversity as a strength |
| Numbers: "105 students", "15%" | "Tons of people", "huge growth" | Concreteness (7Cs) |

## 6. Case Deliverable Structure (The Deck)

### 6.1 The storyline (Standard)
MCC teams compete on **situational analysis → strategy → impact → delivery**. That matches the judging criteria used across Thai case competitions (e.g., CA-ASE's *Situational Analysis / Strategy Recommendations / Impacts / Presentation*). Build every deck on this spine:

| # | Section | Purpose | Slides (of ~20–30) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Title** | Team name (*[X] Consulting*), case title, date. **No university name or logo** where the rules forbid it. | 1 |
| 1 | **Executive Summary** | The full answer on one slide. Use **SCR** (Situation → Complication → Resolution), 3 recommendations, the headline financial impact. | 1 |
| 2 | **Situation & Diagnosis** | The problem as the client sees it, plus root causes. Market, customer, competitor, and company (the 4Cs), with data on every slide. | 3–5 |
| 3 | **Key Issues / Objectives** | A MECE issue tree and a **SMART** objective ("Grow X from A to B by Q4 2027"). | 1–2 |
| 4 | **Options & Evaluation** | 2–4 options scored against explicit criteria (impact, feasibility, cost, risk), and why the chosen option wins. | 1–2 |
| 5 | **Recommendation** | 2–3 strategic pillars. Each pillar gets a *what / how / why it works* slide. | 4–8 |
| 6 | **Implementation Roadmap** | Phased Gantt (Quick wins → Build → Scale), owners, milestones, KPIs. | 1–2 |
| 7 | **Financials** | Investment, revenue/cost impact, **ROI / NPV / payback**, key assumptions stated, sensitivity (base / best / worst). | 2–3 |
| 8 | **Risks & Mitigation** | A likelihood × impact matrix, with a mitigation and owner for each risk. | 1 |
| 9 | **Conclusion / Next Steps** | Restate the answer and the first 90 days. | 1 |
| A | **Appendix** | Calculations, survey methodology, extra data. These slides are your Q&A ammunition. | Unlimited |

### 6.2 Slide-level rules (Standard)
- **Action titles:** every title is a full-sentence takeaway of two lines or fewer.
  - ❌ *"Market Analysis"*
  - ✅ *"Gen Z travelers drive 62% of cross-border card spend, yet the brand captures only 8%"*
- **One message per slide.** If the title needs "and," split the slide.
- **A source on every chart** (bottom-left, small font), plus stated assumptions.
- **Consistent units:** THB with thousands separators (e.g., THB 1,250,000 or THB 1.25M), percentages to one decimal place at most.
- **Read the title and the chart takeaway together.** The judge should get the point in under five seconds.
- **Keep the visual system consistent:** one font family, a 2–3 color palette plus one accent for "the answer," and aligned grids.
- **Tracker / breadcrumb:** a small section marker (e.g., *Diagnosis › Strategy › Impact*) shows the judge where they are.
- **Obey the format rules:** e.g., 16:9, slide caps, PDF naming (`TeamName_Submission.pdf`), one language only, and no institution identifiers when required.

### 6.3 Offline, time-boxed cases (HSBC format)
The HSBC final is a **three-hour case with no internet**, so the standard shifts:
- **Framework-first:** agree on the issue tree in the first 15–20 minutes.
- **Back-of-the-envelope math** with **explicitly labeled assumptions** ("Assuming 30% adoption among existing card holders…").
- **Time split** (suggested): 20% structure, 45% analysis and content, 25% slides, 10% rehearsal.
- **Fewer, sharper slides.** Clarity beats volume.

## 7. Presentation & Q&A Standards

### Delivery (Standard, aligned to common competition rules)
- **Business attire.** Arrive or log in early (competition rules often require 5–10 minutes).
- **Typical slot: 15-minute presentation, 10-minute Q&A, 5-minute judge feedback.** Rehearse to 14 minutes.
- **Open:** "Good morning, esteemed judges. We are **[Team] Consulting**. Today we will show how **[Client]** can **[headline outcome]**."
- **Handoffs:** name the next speaker and topic ("…I'll now hand over to Aileen, who will walk you through our financials.").
- **Close:** restate the answer and impact, then "Thank you. We welcome your questions."
- **Use one language** throughout the slides and speech when the rules require it.

### Q&A method: **A-R-E**
1. **Answer** directly in the first sentence, with a number if possible.
2. **Reason:** one or two supporting points, pointing to an appendix slide ("as shown in Appendix 4").
3. **End:** tie it back to the recommendation.

- Thank the judge for difficult questions ("Thank you, that's an important point"), then answer. Don't be defensive.
- **Spread the answers.** More than half the team should respond during Q&A.
- If you don't know, say so and reason aloud: "We didn't model that directly. Based on our assumption of X, we'd expect Y."

## 10. Pre-Publish Checklist

**Every communication**
- [ ] The right register for the channel (News / Social / Case / Correspondence)
- [ ] Full names and roles are correct and spelled right, with "Khun" where appropriate
- [ ] Dates, venues, and numbers are verified
- [ ] Partners are named correctly and credited
- [ ] Other teams and universities are congratulated respectfully
- [ ] The call to action is clear, with a deadline or link
- [ ] The contact block or social directory is included

**Every deck**
- [ ] The executive summary answers the question on one slide
- [ ] Every title is an action title, with one message per slide
- [ ] Every number is sourced or has a stated assumption
- [ ] Financials show investment, return, and payback, with a sensitivity case
- [ ] Risks are paired with mitigations and owners
- [ ] Competition rules are met (slide cap, 16:9, file name, language, no institution identifiers)
- [ ] Rehearsed to time, with every member ready for Q&A
